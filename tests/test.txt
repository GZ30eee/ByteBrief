Every morning, fourteen-year-old Arjun rode his bicycle past an old railway station on his way to school. The station had been closed for years, but Arjun often stopped to look at its faded signs and broken clock. One rainy morning, he noticed a small wooden box hidden beneath a bench. Inside was a collection of handwritten letters, all addressed to someone named Meera. Curious, Arjun took the box home and carefully read the first letter.

The letters had been written more than thirty years earlier by a young man named Dev, who had left the town to find work in another city. He had promised Meera that he would return, but his letters suddenly stopped after several months. Arjun became determined to discover what had happened. He asked his history teacher about the railway station and learned that many families had moved away when the nearby factory closed. With the teacher's help, Arjun searched old newspapers and eventually found an article mentioning Dev, who had become an engineer and moved to another state.

Arjun continued searching and discovered that Meera was still living in a small village several hundred kilometers away. He sent her a copy of the letters through a local community group. A few weeks later, Meera visited Arjun's town with her granddaughter. She explained that she had never received Dev's final letters because her family had moved suddenly after a flood. The letters in the box had apparently been hidden at the station by a railway worker who had promised to deliver them but never got the chance.

Meera thanked Arjun for finding the letters, saying that they had finally answered questions she had carried for decades. Before leaving, she gave Arjun an old photograph of the railway station and wrote a short note on its back: "Sometimes, the smallest discoveries can reconnect the longest-lost stories." Arjun returned to the station the next morning and placed the photograph inside the empty ticket window. The building was still abandoned, but to him it no longer felt forgotten.
